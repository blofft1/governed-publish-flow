Standard approvals first, Workfront for advanced needs

Principle: do as much as possible in Experience Workspace out of the box. A route is moved to Workfront only when a requirement goes past what standard approvals include. Moving to Workfront changes who does the review work, not the gate, the status rail or the record.

Tier 1: auto-approve when blocking checks pass.More than 3 stages, or a different number of stages depending on the content (conditional routing).

Reminders by email; four-eyes; decision bound to the exact version; withdraw on edit.An audit or compliance process that must live in the customer's work-management system of record.

Who owns what. EW always owns the gate: it holds the version, checks that the decision is for that exact version, enforces four-eyes, publishes, and writes the record. The provider (native or Workfront) owns the review work and returns a decision. Workfront never publishes. Using Workfront is a policy setting per tier or zone, chosen in onboarding, not something an author decides per request. The author sees the same status rail either way.

What this changes in the design basis

  • Standard approvals are bounded, not zero: up to 3 stages, parallel groups (all-of / any-of), reminders, four-eyes and version binding are built into EW. Anything beyond that moves to Workfront.
  • The build ask has two layers: (1) the gate and provider contract, which both paths need; (2) a bounded native stage engine, much smaller than a general workflow engine.
  • Onboarding step 4 becomes "approvals": the agent asks questions, recommends standard or advanced (Workfront) per tier against the ceiling above, and for advanced connects Workfront, picks or creates the template, maps groups to Workfront teams, and runs a test round trip.
